# Literature foundation for insect life-table analysis

Use this reference when choosing a demographic route, validating formulas, interpreting bootstrap output, or describing how the v1 implementation differs from a full age-stage, two-sex analysis.

## 1. Method identity and v1 fidelity

The age-stage, two-sex framework was introduced to retain both sexes and individual variation in stage duration instead of collapsing insects into a female-only, non-overlapping stage schedule. The raw-history implementation described by Chi (1988) derives age-stage survival and fecundity from every member of the initial cohort.

The bundled `life_table.py` currently implements the **two-sex cohort `lx-mx` core**:

- all members of the initial cohort remain in the denominator, including males and individuals that die before adulthood;
- individual survival histories and age-specific reproductive records are collapsed to `lx`, `mx`, and `lx*mx`;
- `R0`, `r`, `lambda`, `T`, and doubling time are calculated from those curves;
- complete individual histories can be resampled as whole units.

It does **not** yet produce a complete age-stage, two-sex analysis. Do not claim equivalence to TWOSEX-MSChart because v1 does not calculate or export:

- age-stage survival `s_xj` or age-stage fecundity `f_xj`;
- stage-overlap curves or stable age-stage distributions;
- age-stage life expectancy `e_xj` or reproductive value `v_xj`;
- group-reared or bootstrap-matched life tables;
- population projection or survival-weighted consumption/parasitism parameters;
- formal paired-bootstrap or Cartesian-product treatment tests.

Use the label `two-sex cohort lx-mx core analysis` in methods and reports. Use `full age-stage, two-sex life table` only when stage-specific outputs and their assumptions are actually implemented or generated by a validated external tool.

## 2. Core definitions and equations

Let `N0` be the complete initial cohort, `x` an integer age beginning at zero, and `b_ix` the confirmed offspring count produced by individual `i` during age interval `x`.

```text
lx       = number from N0 alive at age x / N0
lx * mx  = sum_i(b_ix) / N0
mx       = (lx * mx) / lx, when lx > 0
R0       = sum_x(lx * mx)
1        = sum_x(exp(-r * (x + 1)) * lx * mx)
lambda   = exp(r)
T        = ln(R0) / r, when defined
DT       = ln(2) / r, only for r > 0
```

The discrete Euler-Lotka equation uses `x + 1` when age is indexed from zero. Normalize the source time convention explicitly: a source column called `day 1` may represent age interval zero, but never subtract one without confirming the observation protocol.

Required consistency checks:

- `R0 = sum(lx*mx)` within numerical tolerance;
- `lambda = exp(r)`;
- `r > 0`, `= 0`, or `< 0` should correspond to `R0 > 1`, `= 1`, or `< 1` when `R0 > 0` and the same time convention is used;
- `T = ln(R0)/r` only where the quotient is defined;
- `r` has units of inverse time, `T` and `DT` have units of time, `lambda` is the multiplication factor per time step, and `R0` is offspring per initial individual under the confirmed offspring definition.

Reproduction must be attached to the reproductive parent at the parent's age. Record whether `fecundity` means eggs, hatched progeny, emerged adults, daughters, or all offspring. A pooled sex ratio is not a safe substitute when offspring sex ratio changes with maternal age.

The core calculation treats an unlisted individual-age fecundity event as zero. Because an absent row can instead mean a missed observation, `life_table.py` requires the explicit `--confirm-unlisted-fecundity-zero` acknowledgement. Do not pass it until the table design or study record confirms that unlisted events are observed or structural zero.

## 3. Cohort and endpoint requirements

Use the core demographic route only if:

- the initial cohort is identifiable and includes every enrolled individual;
- one time origin applies to survival and reproduction, or conversions are documented;
- deaths are distinguished from escape, loss, technical failure, and right censoring;
- reproduction is observed by age, not only as an undated lifetime total;
- treatment and independent experimental unit are known.

The current script requires complete death endpoints. A loss or an individual still alive at study end is right-censored, not dead. Route such data to a censoring-aware survival analysis rather than inserting the last observation as `death_date`.

## 4. Bootstrap decisions

Resample complete biological histories, never individual days or offspring. If individuals were independently assigned and reared, the individual is the natural sampling unit. If treatment was applied to cages, mothers, blocks, cohorts, or runs, use a cluster/biological-replicate bootstrap and describe it as such; do not present within-cluster individuals as independent replicates.

Prefer bootstrap over jackknife for these nonlinear demographic quantities. Literature in this method family has shown that jackknife variance estimation can be invalid for `R0` and associated parameters.

Bootstrap sample count is a precision choice, not a universal ritual:

- use a fixed seed and save the requested number of resamples;
- for final reporting, compare interval limits across at least two increasing resample counts;
- 100,000 resamples are common in TWOSEX-MSChart studies, while smaller values may be useful during exploration;
- report the number and fraction of finite/calculable results for every metric.

An infertile resample can have `R0 = 0`, leaving `r`, `lambda`, and `T` undefined. Dropping those resamples makes their reported percentile interval conditional on calculable samples and can be misleading when the fraction is appreciable. The v1 script therefore reports `noncalculable_resamples` and `ci_scope`; it does not impute an arbitrary value. Do not use a conditional interval for formal treatment inference. Escalate high or scientifically consequential noncalculable fractions to a method review or a validated full implementation.

## 5. Parasitoids, predators, and host impact

Keep demographic fitness and biocontrol performance separate.

- `R0`, `r`, `lambda`, and `T` describe population replacement and timing.
- Parasitism, direct host killing, emergence, and female proportion describe observed performance with their own denominators.
- A demographic net consumption or net parasitism rate requires age/stage-specific performance integrated with survival, analogous to `C0 = sum_x sum_j(s_xj*c_xj)` in predator studies.

The bundled `parasitoid_metrics.py` provides descriptive ratios and totals. It does not calculate a survival-weighted net predation/parasitism rate or a transformation rate. Never rename its pooled event summaries as `C0`, `Qp`, or a full CONSUME-MSChart analysis.

1. Chi, H., & Liu, H. (1985). *Two new methods for the study of insect population ecology*. Bulletin of the Institute of Zoology, Academia Sinica, 24(2), 225-240. [Full-text record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242218411_Two_new_methods_for_study_of_insect_population_ecology).
   Basis for age-stage structure, inclusion of both sexes, and individual variation in development.

2. Chi, H. (1988). *Life-table analysis incorporating both sexes and variable development rates among individuals*. Environmental Entomology, 17(1), 26-34. [doi:10.1093/ee/17.1.26](https://doi.org/10.1093/ee/17.1.26).
   Basis for deriving age-stage, two-sex quantities from raw individual life histories.

3. Chi, H., You, M., Atlıhan, R., et al. (2020). *Age-stage, two-sex life table: an introduction to theory, data analysis, and application*. Entomologia Generalis, 40(2), 103-124. [doi:10.1127/entomologia/2020/0936](https://doi.org/10.1127/entomologia/2020/0936).
   Modern synthesis of notation, equations, interpretation, and applications.

4. Huang, Y.-B., & Chi, H. (2012). *Age-stage, two-sex life tables of Bactrocera cucurbitae ... with a discussion on the problem of applying female age-specific life tables to insect populations*.    Empirical comparison showing how female-only aggregation can mishandle preadult mortality and variable development.

5. Huang, Y.-B., & Chi, H. (2013). *Life tables of Bactrocera cucurbitae (Diptera: Tephritidae): with an invalidation of the jackknife technique*. Journal of Applied Entomology, 137(5), 327-339. [doi:10.1111/jen.12002](https://doi.org/10.1111/jen.12002).

7. Chi, H., Güncan, A., Kavousi, A., et al. (2022). *TWOSEX-MSChart: the key tool for life table research and education*. Entomologia Generalis, 42(6), 845-849. [doi:10.1127/entomologia/2022/1851](https://doi.org/10.1127/entomologia/2022/1851).
